# Notice-standard references

Public sources behind `base.yaml` (universal) and `india.yaml` (IN overlay).
All figures are **unvalidated** until a QS signs off (Doc §2.4, §14). Day-counts
vary by contract edition and particular conditions — these are checklist
prompts, not legal advice.

## Universal (base.yaml)

- **FIDIC Red Book 1999, Sub-Clause 20.1** — contractor's claim notice "as soon
  as practicable and not later than **28 days**" after awareness; late notice is
  time-barred.
- **FIDIC Red Book 2017, Sub-Clause 20.2** — Notice of Claim within **28 days**;
  fully-detailed claim (with contractual/legal basis) within **84 days**. Both
  are conditions precedent to entitlement.
- **NEC4 ECC, Clause 61.3** — contractor notifies a compensation event within
  **8 weeks (56 days)** of becoming aware, else no change to Prices / Completion
  Date / Key Dates (a strict time bar).
- **JCT SBC/Q 2016** — extension-of-time notice "forthwith"; loss-and-expense
  application "as soon as … it becomes apparent" (no fixed day-count).

## India (india.yaml)

- **CPWD GCC (2020)** — running-account-bill claim practice; **Clause 10CC**
  price escalation on works running beyond a stated period (frequently deleted
  in tenders).
- **NHAI / MoRTH standard conditions** — FIDIC-based, with particular-conditions
  amendments to the notice regimes.
- **MSMED Act 2006, Section 15** — payment to a registered micro/small
  enterprise within the agreed date but never beyond **45 days** of acceptance
  (**15 days** if no written term); a term beyond 45 days is void to that
  extent; delayed payment attracts compound interest at 3× the RBI bank rate.

## Verified (July 2026)

The FIDIC 28/84-day, NEC 8-week, and MSMED 45-day figures were cross-checked
against public commentary during the build; citations above name the primary
clause/section. A QS must still confirm applicability per contract before these
are marked `confidence: validated`.
